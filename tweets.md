How did transnationalism materialize in pre-Communist China? How did non-state organizations exploit contemporary mass media to shape new and competing conceptions of the public? How can historians leverage computational methods to harness vast collections of digitized newspapers? Using the Rotary Club as a case study, this article analyses extensive collections of Chinese and English periodicals to reveal how this transnational organization promoted itself as a force for social welfare and international peace in early 20th-century China. Readers will gain inspiration on how to combine methods like topic modeling, named entity recognition, geographical information system, and network analysis to uncover significant variations in public discourses and practices across languages and over time.
